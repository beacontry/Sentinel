Competitive landscape

Trendspider (overlapping technicals + alerts) and Composer.trade (overlapping no-code strategy + Alpaca execution). These are the two products you're most likely to be compared with on a sales call.
02

What Beacontry has that competitors don't

Real differentiators, ordered roughly by retail-trader impact (refreshed 2026-05-26 with PR 8/14/16/17 capabilities):

  1. Hybrid signal pipeline in one place — technical + sentiment + options flow + analyst consensus + AI scoring + Reddit chatter, all feeding one decision. Trade Ideas does AI but black-box. Trendspider does technicals only. Benzinga does news only. Nobody integrates all five layers at the retail price point.
  2. Bring-your-own-broker — multi-broker (Alpaca, Tradier, IBKR), each user supplies their own keys. Trade Ideas couples to TradeStation; Tickeron pushes its broker. Most trading-intel tools are quasi-broker-affiliated; we're not.
  3. Per-symbol GA-tuned strategy with multi-objective fitness — Beacontry's optimizer evolves strategy params per symbol (not a one-size-fits-all preset) and scores candidates against a blended fitness of return × Sharpe-multiplier × drawdown-multiplier, not raw return alone. Result: parameters that survive volatility, not just look good in a backtest. QuantConnect has GA but requires Python coding; nobody at the retail price exposes it as a one-click "optimize." PR 16 added multi-obj fitness with sign-correct inversion and drawdown-gradient fix.
  4. Take-profit graduation (post-PR-8) — when a winning position crosses its take-profit threshold, Beacontry doesn't hard-exit like every preset-driven competitor. The protective stop ratchets to entry × 1.30 (a +30% floor) and the position keeps running until 2-of-3 weakness signals fire (volume contraction, price plateau, RSI rollover). This is the difference between a +12% closed gain and a +42% closed gain on a DELL-style runner — and most retail auto-trading tools have no equivalent mechanism at all.
  5. Swap-sell post-exit redeploy (post-PR-14) — when an exit fires mid-scan, the freed slot is filled same-scan by the next-best deferred STRONG_BUY rather than sitting idle until the next 15-min tick. Capital efficiency competitors don't match, especially in strong markets where opportunity is dense.
  6. Adaptive engine mode (regime-driven) — VIX + SPY trend switches strategy automatically between conservative / moderate / optimized / aggressive base modes. Nobody else exposes this as an end-user feature — it's institutional fund stuff dressed for retail.
  7. Audit-grade compliance built in — hash-chained audit log (every privileged action, every order, every config rotation), MFA, encrypted credentials at rest (AES-256-GCM), per-user risk profile, statement timeouts on every query. Most retail trading tools have NO audit trail at all — Beacontry would be the only retail option that could pass an enterprise security review.
  8. Live-trading safeguard stack — account-switch detection, equity-collapse halt, broker-disconnect halt (after 5 consecutive failures), order rate limiting (30/min), daily notional caps, consecutive-loss auto-halt, PDT-aware buy blocking, wash-sale protection (with §475(f) MTM exemption), client_order_id idempotency on every order, 5-min broker-stop reconciliation independent of scan health, PDT-rejected-exit suppression with push notification. Retail tools assume "if you set it up, you accept the risk." Beacontry has circuit breakers and reconciliation. PRs 1–9 + 14–17 hardened this stack on 2026-05-26.
  9. Trader Tax Status + MTM + wash-sale tracking — automated. Tax Center merges manual portfolios + engine trades + lot cost basis. Competitors leave you with TaxAct + a spreadsheet. Niche-defining feature for active traders crossing 3+ day-trades per week.
  10. Education + spaced-repetition + AI quizzes baked into the same platform — 14 typed guides, 8 calculators, 95 glossary terms, SM-2 algorithm. Nobody combines education + execution like this.
  11. Journal v2 with auto-stub + daily prompts + AI weekly review — auto-creates entries on filled trades, pre/post-market prompts, Sunday AI recap. TraderSync nails journal; Beacontry matches it AND ties entries to live engine trades.
  12. Official-source Congressional trades — daily ingest of the House Clerk PTR archive + Senate efdsearch directly. Free across all tiers. Quiver charges $10–50/mo for the same data with a 1-day lag; Beacontry has no lag and no extra cost.
  13. Five themes including colorblind mode — minor, but polish nobody else bothers with.
03

What competitors have that Beacontry doesn't

Honest about the gaps — these are what's holding adoption back:

  1. Massive existing community — TradingView has 50M+ users. Trade Ideas has an active chatroom. Beacontry's community is empty (no critical mass to drive forum/feed/DM activity).
  2. Mobile-native apps — TradingView/TOS/Webull have polished iOS+Android apps. Beacontry is web/PWA. Mobile traders won't try a web-only tool.
  3. Real-time L2 / order-book data — competitors paying for SIP feeds. Beacontry is on Yahoo/Finnhub free tiers — delayed quotes, no Level 2.
  4. Crypto support — no Binance/Coinbase integration. Half the retail trading market is now crypto.
  5. Options analytics — Tastytrade, TOS, Tradier have full option chains, Greeks, P&L diagrams. Beacontry touches options minimally.
  6. Fractional shares — Alpaca supports them; Beacontry doesn't pass through to fractional orders yet. Excludes the "$5 of NVDA at a $1k+ share price" retail demographic. Logged in docs/future-ideas.md.
  7. Established brand + track record — competitors have years of marketing, case studies, testimonials. You have zero name recognition.
  8. Customer support team — competitors have ticket queues, live chat, sales reps. You're solo-built.
  9. Free tier subsidized by order flow — Robinhood/Webull/eToro give away their entire platform because they sell order flow. You can't compete on free.
  10. Heat maps, market profile, advanced charting libraries — TradingView's chart engine is best-in-class. You embed it (smart) but if they raise embed pricing or rate-limit, you're exposed.
  11. Integrations marketplace — Zapier, Slack, Discord (you have Discord — partial credit), TradingView alerts, etc. Most competitors have integration libraries.
  12. Established backtest dataset depth — competitors have years of tick data, alt-data backtests. Yahoo daily + Alpaca paper history is good but not deep.

Pricing recommendation

Beacontry sits in the mid-premium tier of retail trading-intel tools. Cheaper than Trade Ideas / Trendspider Premium, more capable than Stock Rover / TradingView Pro. Concrete tiering:

Recommended tier structure

Pricing rationale

  • $29/mo Trader tier is the sweet spot: cheaper than Trendspider Essential ($48), competitive with TradingView Pro ($30), but strictly more in execution + journal + tax + AI. Easy "I'll try it" price.
  • $79/mo Pro undercuts Trade Ideas Standard ($84) while shipping the GA optimizer + adaptive mode that competitors charge $150–228/mo for.
  • Annual discount of 17% (≈2 months free) is standard SaaS — reduces churn dramatically.
  • Free self-hosted is your moat against Trade Ideas / Composer — they CAN'T copy that without giving up their business model.
  • Don't ship a pure-free hosted tier — your costs (Finnhub API, server, Alpaca, Anthropic for AI scoring) make free uneconomic. The Free tier is essentially a demo for marketing.
What NOT to charge for separately
Don't charge per-data-source (Congress, sentiment, etc.) — bundle. Quiver charges $10/mo for Congress alone; you commodify them all together. Don't paywall education — it's marketing. Don't paywall the journal — journal users churn less.

Marketing strategy

Positioning

Lead with one of these three positioning angles. Pick the one that feels truest:

  1. "The transparent alternative to black-box AI signal tools" — direct contrast with Trade Ideas / Tickeron. Every signal shows its math. Every hybrid layer is inspectable. Pitch to traders burned by AI tools that didn't deliver.
  2. "Built for the trader who wants to know exactly what fired" — emphasizes audit trail, hash-chained log, the "Signal DNA" feature. Pitch to professional retail (6-fig accounts, day-traders, PDT-qualified).
  3. "The Quantopian successor that respects your data" — exploits the Quantopian-shutdown orphan market (still angry 4 years later). Open code, self-hostable, BYO broker. Pitch to dev-curious traders.
My recommendation
#3 first, #1 second. The Quantopian crowd is your warmest audience and they're vocal on Twitter / Reddit.

Launch sequence (90 days)

Days 1–14: Foundation

  • Pick a name + buy the domain (after the brainstorm closes)
  • Build a marketing site (1 page) — hero, 6-feature grid, pricing, "join waitlist" form. Don't overthink it. Vercel free tier.
  • Basic SEO — schema markup, Open Graph cards, robots.txt
  • Open-source the engine on GitHub (or at least make a public mirror). README with screenshots.
  • Set up a Discord server. Add the existing market-digest webhook to seed activity.

Days 15–30: Show, don't tell

  • Show HN: I built an open-source trading intelligence platform — this is your launch. Hacker News is exactly your target dev-curious trader. Aim for front page; expect 30–50K visits if it lands.
  • Cross-post to r/algotrading + r/quantfinance + r/datascience. Lead with the GA optimizer + adaptive mode. Avoid r/wallstreetbets (wrong audience).
  • Long-form Substack: "I built a trading platform with a hash-chained audit log; here's why retail tools need this." Cross-post to Medium.

Days 31–60: Establish credibility

  • Public paper-trading transparency — run the engine on a paper account, post weekly P&L + actual signal log (with "Signal DNA"). Be honest about losses. Hide live account. Costs nothing, builds enormous trust.
  • Twitter / X presence — daily "Beacontry saw X today" thread. FinTwit is where retail traders actually live.
  • YouTube: 1 video — "I built a trading bot that uses Reddit chatter + Congress trades + technical signals." 15–20 min long. Show the dashboard, click through, narrate.
  • Outreach: comment substantively on 5 high-traffic FinTwit accounts per day. Don't shill — engage.

Days 61–90: Convert

  • Open beta — invite-only at first (use the existing invite system), small cohort. Get 50 active users. Free during beta, $29/mo after 30 days.
  • Beta users post screenshots → social proof.
  • Case study: "Beta user X grew their paper account 14% in 60 days using adaptive mode + Reddit sentiment." Real numbers.
  • Referral program: 1 month free for each successful signup. Most underpriced growth channel for SaaS under $50/mo.

Channels — order of likely ROI

  1. Hacker News launch — best first move; one good Show HN can drive 10K+ signups
  2. Twitter / X (FinTwit) — daily presence, engagement-led, slow burn but compounds
  3. YouTube long-form — 1 video can drive months of conversions if it ranks
  4. Reddit (algotrading / quantfinance / securityanalysis) — value-first posts, never shilling
  5. Substack + cross-post — long-form positioning, less viral but high-quality leads
  6. Referral program — built-in viral coefficient
  7. GitHub stars — passive signal of credibility for engineering audience
  8. Paid ads — DON'T start here. Skip for the first 6 months. ROI on Google Ads for fintech tools below $50/mo is brutal.

What NOT to do

No paid ads in first 6 months No content gating No "80% win rate!" claims Don't price below $20/mo Don't ignore journal/education in marketing

Don't price too low to "compete" — $9.99/mo positions you as cheap, not premium. The premium fee is what makes the audit/compliance pitch credible. Sophisticated audiences smell wild win-rate claims and walk.
